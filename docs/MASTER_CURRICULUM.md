# TOEIC Master Curriculum — Product & Content Standard

**Status:** canonical target curriculum; this document defines intended coverage, not a claim that every item is already implemented.
**Product direction:** TOEIC-first, beginner-to-advanced, four-skill English learning with TOEIC test preparation.
**Primary learner journey:** Pre-A1 → A1 → A2 → B1 → B2 → C1. C1+ workplace specialization is an extension, not a CEFR level.

## 1. Product promise and scope

The product combines two complementary outcomes:

1. **Learn English from zero:** guided progression from sound/letter recognition through everyday communication and independent learning.
2. **Prepare for TOEIC and use English at work:** structured Listening & Reading practice, optional Speaking & Writing preparation, test simulation, and workplace communication.

TOEIC Listening & Reading is the primary exam track. TOEIC Speaking & Writing is a complementary track. General English foundations are prerequisites and learning support, not a claim that TOEIC itself tests every language skill. IELTS remains deferred until the TOEIC curriculum and development backlog are completed and the owner approves a phase change.

## 2. Proficiency stages and outcomes

| Stage | Main outcome | Required learning scope |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-A1 | Recognize and imitate basic English | Alphabet, letter names, sound awareness, phonics foundations, numbers, classroom instructions, basic nouns/verbs, listening imitation |
| A1 | Understand and produce simple familiar sentences | Basic sentence patterns, be/have/do, pronouns, articles, present simple/continuous, basic questions, daily-life vocabulary, short dialogues |
| A2 | Handle routine daily situations | Past/future forms, present perfect introduction, modals, comparisons, quantifiers, basic passive/relative clauses, short messages and emails |
| B1 | Operate independently in familiar study/work contexts | Full core tense system, word forms, agreement, pronouns, clauses, passive, conditionals, reported speech, TOEIC task familiarity, multi-turn conversations and workplace documents |
| B2 | Understand complex information and communicate independently | Advanced clause/verb patterns, inference, paraphrase, multiple documents, extended listening, professional emails/reports, structured speaking and writing |
| C1 | Use English flexibly in complex professional contexts | Nuance, register, complex argument, long-form listening/reading, presentations, negotiation, reports and specialized workplace communication |
| C1+ extension | Apply English in specialized high-stakes contexts | Cross-cultural collaboration, leadership, negotiation, policy/procedure writing and domain-specific communication; not a formal CEFR band |

Placement must be diagnostic, not age-based. Adult beginners may start at Pre-A1. Do not force learners to complete material they have already mastered; diagnostic evidence may recommend a higher entry point.

## 3. Grammar coverage matrix

Every grammar topic must include: learning objective, plain-language explanation, form/structure, usage, positive/negative/question patterns where applicable, examples with Vietnamese support, common errors, contrast with confusing forms, guided practice, independent practice, answer rationale and spaced review.

### 3.1 Foundations and sentence building
- Alphabet, capitalization, spelling and basic punctuation.
- Parts of speech and word order.
- Subject, verb, object, complement and modifiers.
- Be, have, do; action and linking verbs.
- Singular/plural, countable/uncountable and possessive nouns.
- Articles, determiners, demonstratives and quantifiers.
- Personal, object, possessive, reflexive, demonstrative, indefinite and relative pronouns; pronoun reference/agreement.
- Adjectives, adverbs, order, position, degree and comparison.
- Prepositions of time/place/movement and dependent prepositions.
- Questions, negatives, imperatives, requests and question tags.

### 3.2 Core verb system
- Present Simple and Present Continuous.
- Past Simple and Past Continuous.
- Future forms: will, be going to, present continuous for arrangements, present simple for schedules.
- Present Perfect and Past Perfect; contrast with simple past.
- Future Continuous and Future Perfect where level-appropriate.
- Perfect Continuous forms where level-appropriate.
- Tense consistency, time markers, stative verbs and aspect.
- Subject–Verb Agreement, including compound/indefinite subjects and proximity patterns.
- Modal verbs: ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice, deduction and expectation.
- Gerunds, infinitives, bare infinitives, verb patterns and meaning changes.
- Participles, participial phrases and reduced clauses.

### 3.3 Complex sentences and meaning
- Coordinating, subordinating and correlative conjunctions.
- Noun, adjective/relative and adverb clauses.
- Defining/non-defining and reduced relative clauses.
- Passive voice across common tenses, modal passive and reporting structures.
- Comparatives, superlatives, parallel comparisons and modifiers.
- Zero, first, second, third and mixed conditionals; unless/provided/as long as.
- Reported statements, questions, commands and tense/backshift rules.
- Direct/indirect questions, embedded questions and question tags.
- Connectors, discourse markers, cause/effect, contrast, concession and sequence.
- Complex noun phrases, appositives, prepositional phrases and modifiers.

### 3.4 Advanced grammar and mechanics
- Inversion, emphasis, cleft sentences and fronting.
- Subjunctive and formal recommendation/requirement structures.
- Ellipsis, substitution, parallelism and coordination.
- Advanced conditionals and modal perfect forms.
- Register-sensitive grammar and formal/informal usage.
- Punctuation: comma, semicolon, colon, apostrophe, quotation marks and sentence boundaries.

### 3.5 TOEIC grammar application
- Word Forms; Tenses and Aspect; Passive Voice; Subject–Verb Agreement; Pronouns; Conjunctions and Prepositions; Relative Clauses.
- Articles/Determiners, Nouns, Adjectives/Adverbs, Modals, Gerunds/Infinitives, Participles, Verb Patterns, Comparisons, Conditionals and sentence connectors.
- Part 5 sentence completion and Part 6 text completion applications.

### 4.3 Vocabulary record quality
- Stable unique `id`, canonical lemma and spelling variants where justified.
- One or more senses with POS, Vietnamese meaning and concise English definition.
- US IPA as the current primary pronunciation; add UK IPA only with reliable evidence.
- Natural examples with translations; examples must demonstrate the target sense.
- Word family links only when derivational relationship and POS are correct.
- Verb forms for irregular/important verbs; do not fill irrelevant fields.
- Collocations and phrasal verbs with usage context; no mechanically generated lists.
- Synonyms/antonyms only when meaning and grammatical role match.
- CEFR and TOEIC relevance are editorial estimates until reviewed; label them honestly.
- Topic tags, difficulty, frequency/priority and links to lessons/questions.
- Distinguish headword count, sense count and relationship-record count in reports.

Internal planning target: 3,000–5,000 useful headwords for the foundational TOEIC-oriented lexicon, then expand based on topic gaps and learner evidence. This is a project target, not an ETS vocabulary requirement.

## 5. Four-skill learning framework

### Listening
- Sound/word recognition, syllables, stress, weak forms, linking and reductions.
- Short utterances and everyday dialogues; natural speech at graded speeds.
- TOEIC Part 1 photographs; Part 2 question–response; Part 3 conversations; Part 4 talks.
- Main idea, detail, intent, inference, attitude, paraphrase and next action.
- Transcript alignment, replay by segment, dictation, shadowing and error diagnosis.
- Multiple common English accents; clear male/female voices and natural pauses. Browser TTS must be labelled as synthesized, not authentic exam audio.

### Reading
- Letter/word/sentence decoding, grammar in context and paragraph structure.
- Skimming, scanning, main idea, detail, reference, vocabulary in context and inference.
- TOEIC Part 5 incomplete sentences; Part 6 text completion; Part 7 single/multiple passages.
- Emails, notices, forms, advertisements, schedules, invoices, reports, policies, charts and multi-document sets.
- Timed reading, paraphrase recognition and evidence-based answer explanations.

### Speaking
- Pronunciation, intelligibility, stress, rhythm and intonation.
- Greetings, personal information, questions, descriptions and short narratives.
- Workplace requests, updates, clarification, telephone calls and meetings.
- TOEIC Speaking task practice: read aloud, describe a picture, respond to questions, respond using provided information and express an opinion.
- Recording, replay, speech-to-text where available and rubric-based feedback. Do not claim official ETS scoring.

### Writing
- Spelling, capitalization, punctuation, sentence construction and paragraphing.
- Messages, invitations, confirmations, requests and everyday correspondence.
- Workplace email, complaint/response, explanation, summary, report and proposal.
- TOEIC Writing task practice: build sentences from pictures, respond to written requests and write an opinion essay.
- Feedback for task fulfillment, organization, grammar, vocabulary, tone and mechanics. Do not claim official ETS scoring.

## 6. Workplace and life communication scenarios

Use integrated scenarios rather than isolated word lists: introductions; shopping and appointments; travel; education/training; office communication; HR/recruitment; meetings; telephone; email; schedules/events; purchasing/quotes/orders; invoices/payments; customer service; shipping/logistics; IT support; facilities/maintenance; reporting; presentations; negotiation; project coordination; compliance and cross-cultural communication.

Each scenario should connect relevant vocabulary, grammar, listening/reading source material and an output task (speaking or writing).

## 7. Learning methodology and learner support

- Diagnostic placement and transparent recommended entry level.
- Small lessons with one measurable objective and prerequisite links.
- Teach → guided example → controlled practice → independent practice → recall/review.
- Adaptive difficulty based on demonstrated mastery, not only streaks or total clicks.
- Spaced repetition for vocabulary and concepts; separate recognition from productive recall.
- Error notebook with error type, explanation, retry and later reassessment.
- Audio controls for replay, speed, segment and transcript visibility.
- Accessibility: keyboard operation, readable typography, captions/transcripts, reduced motion and clear feedback.
- Progress by skill, level, topic and objective; distinguish exposure, practice, mastery and assessment evidence.
- Avoid punitive streak mechanics; encourage sustainable learning and recovery after missed days.

## 8. TOEIC exam preparation

Maintain separate exam blueprints from general learning content. L&R practice follows the current official test structure; S&W is a complementary exam track. Use original editorial questions and licensed/owned assets only. Never reproduce ETS protected questions/audio or imply that project mock scores are official/equated scores.

Question banks require stable IDs, one defensible key, plausible but uniquely incorrect distractors, answer-specific explanations, source/evidence where relevant, editorial status and review provenance. Automated tests do not equal independent semantic review.

## 9. Coverage and completion rules

Coverage is tracked at objective/lesson/question level with these statuses: `missing`, `partial`, `in-progress`, `review-needed`, `complete`. A topic is complete only when its level-appropriate objectives, instruction, examples, guided and independent practice, explanations, review hooks and assessment evidence exist and pass validation. For four-skill outcomes, mark each skill separately. Do not infer complete coverage from raw item counts.

Reports must separate:
- implemented content vs planned curriculum;
- structural validation vs editorial review vs independent review;
- practice-only reserve vs Mock-eligible content;
- headwords vs senses vs questions vs learning objectives;
- local test results vs remote CI vs deployment/device testing.

## 10. Release governance

- TOEIC-first remains fixed; IELTS is deferred.
- Preserve stable IDs, IndexedDB database/store and learner progress unless an explicitly reviewed migration is required.
- Reuse shared UI and engines; do not fork a feature-specific copy when an existing abstraction fits.
- Refactor incrementally behind regression tests; do not rewrite stable modules for cosmetic reasons.
- GitHub-only static PWA; no backend/service expansion without owner approval.
- Keep one canonical curriculum, one Master status, one previous-version note, one current-version note and one consolidated project history.
- At each release, current becomes previous; retire older version-specific notes after summarizing material facts into `PROJECT_HISTORY.md`.
